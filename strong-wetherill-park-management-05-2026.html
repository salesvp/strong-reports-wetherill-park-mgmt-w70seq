Executive Summary
Current State

Membership: Membership eased in May after two stable months, with total members moving from 247 in March through 248 in April to 240 in May. Active membership dropped to 172, pulling the active rate to 71.67%, while attrition held at 4.47% — inside the sub-7% target for a third consecutive month.

Revenue: Gross revenue declined for a third month, tracking $52,996 in March, $51,747 in April and $49,440 in May, totalling $154,183 across the rolling quarter. Membership revenue held the floor at $41,853 — 85% of May's gross — confirming the base is absorbing continued acquisition softness rather than eroding.

Utilisation: Utilisation remained well below target, with booking easing from 52.98% in March through 46.59% in April to 45.84% in May against the 75–85% optimal range. Attendance closed at 42.73%. The booking–attendance gap held tight at 3.11%, confirming that members who book continue to attend sessions.

Sales vs Cancellations: Membership sales moved from 9 in March to 11 in April before easing to 7 in May against 11 cancellations. Intro offers declined across the period to 21 units, and first visits recovered to 51 from April's 39 but remain materially below March's 72 across the same window.

Key Issues
  • Membership sales declined: May's 7 sales sit below April's 11 and well under the team's demonstrated capability of 21
  • Intro offers still falling: Volume eased from 32 in March to 21 in May despite the extended first purchase offer
  • Paid lead flow absent in May: Ad account access delays kept the external campaign out of market during the report month
  • First visits below March: May's 51 recovered from April's 39 but remains materially short of March's 72
  • Utilisation in the negative band: Booking at 45.84% and attendance at 42.73% sit far below the optimal range
  • Active rate slipped: May's 71.67% fell from April's 76.21%, nearing the bottom of the warning band
Active Interventions Being Deployed
  • 7 Class First Purchase Offer: Extended through the end of May to lower the entry barrier for new prospects and rebuild intro offer volume from May's 21 units
  • Buy 3 Get 5 Lapsed Pack offer: Targeting lapsed clients with bonus pack value to reactivate dormant contacts and support pack volume, which held at 43 units in May
  • External paid ads activation: Ad account access delays held the campaign out of May, with the external marketing team now activating in June to rebuild lead flow
  • Foundation rates last chance campaign: Running 25 May into mid-June to convert price-sensitive prospects before the foundation rate window closes permanently for the studio
  • Mandatory STRONG 25 tracking: Active across the team, systemising first 25 classes retention discipline where the majority of cancellations occur
  • FOH-trainer journey realignment: May all-hands meeting held to address the cohesion gap between front-of-house and trainers across the intro offer handoff
Expected Outcomes
  • Lift first visits: June paid ads activation should rebuild lead flow from May's 51 toward March's 72
  • Rebuild intro offer volume: Extended 7 Class First Purchase Offer should support recovery from May's 21 units
  • Recover membership sales: Foundation rate closure and improved lead flow should support sales lifting above May's 7
  • Hold attrition inside target: STRONG 25 discipline should keep attrition near May's 4.47%, well inside the sub-7% target
  • Restore the active base: Converting suspensions back to active should lift the active rate from 71.67% toward 80%
  • Improve booking utilisation: Added top-of-funnel volume should support booking recovery from 45.84% toward the 75–85% range

Insight

Revenue eased for a third consecutive month, with gross moving from $52,996 in March through $51,747 in April to $49,440 in May — a rolling three-month total of $154,183. Membership revenue remained the dominant stream, tracking $45,648, $43,078 and $41,853 across the period, with May representing 85% of gross. Package revenue held a $5,087–$6,614 baseline, closing at $5,768, while intro offer revenue declined from $1,625 to $1,093.

The membership base continues to hold the revenue floor while acquisition volume stays soft, so the step-down reflects thinner intake rather than base erosion. POS revenue and other revenue sat at $0 across all three months, and gift card revenue registered $55 in May. Fees eased from $636 through $570 to $511. (While late cancel fees contribute to revenue, the goal is not to increase this stream — reduced fees indicate better member attendance behaviour, which drives long-term retention and stickiness.)

Summary & Path Forward

Wetherill Park closed May with retention intact and acquisition still the limiting factor. Total members eased from 247 in March and 248 in April to 240 in May, with attrition at 4.47% — inside the sub-7% target across all three months. Gross revenue tracked $52,996, $51,747 and $49,440, totalling $154,183, with membership revenue holding 85% of May's gross. Booking utilisation closed at 45.84% and the booking–attendance gap stayed tight at 3.11%.

The binding constraint sits upstream in reach rather than in conversion mechanics or member stickiness. With paid ads activating in June once ad account access is resolved, June becomes the first month carrying paid lead flow and the first real test of whether first visits lift above May's 51 and intro offers above 21. Foundation rate closure and the EOFY pack window give June a second conversion lever.

Key Takeaways
  • Retention held across the period with attrition inside the sub-7% target every month, closing May at 4.47%
  • The limiting factor sits upstream in reach — first visits at 51 and intro offers at 21 both remain thin
  • Membership revenue at 85% of gross is holding the revenue floor while intake volume stays soft
  • June carries the 7 Class offer, paid ads activation, foundation rate closure and the EOFY pack window
