EXECUTIVE SUMMARY

Membership

257 total members at end of Q4 2025, with 169 active members (65.8% active rate) and 88 suspended members. Active membership declined from 206 in October to 169 in December, representing a loss of 37 active members across the quarter. However, this decline is not a reflection of service quality—the trainers and team at Wetherill Park have the longest tenure in the network and consistently deliver world-class sessions. Rather, the membership challenges stem from relatively minor but compounding inefficiencies across multiple operational areas: inconsistent social media engagement, gaps in active member community connection, missed opportunities to capture milestones and birthdays, and critically, the absence of intentional acquisition processes and active retention strategies. Individually, these gaps may seem minor; collectively, they create the environment where cancellations occur and new member sign-ups fail to materialise despite strong service delivery.

Revenue

Q4 2025 generated $176,446 in total revenue, with membership revenue representing 78.2% at $138,012. Monthly membership revenue compressed from $50,782 in October to $43,516 in December as active membership declined, demonstrating the direct correlation between member base health and recurring revenue stability. November's $64,537 peak was driven by Black Friday package sales success ($19,467).

Utilisation

Q4 averaged 46.09% utilisation, significantly below the 70-75% target range. Session attendance declined from 2,490 in October to 1,991 in December, reflecting both declining active membership and reduced per-member booking frequency during the holiday season. Substantial capacity opportunity exists within current infrastructure.

Sales vs Cancellations

Q4 produced 23 membership sales against 36 cancellations, resulting in net negative member movement of -13 for the quarter. Monthly performance: October (+3 net), November (-10 net), December (-6 net). Despite 190 first visits across Q4 demonstrating lead generation activity, conversion rates remained well below potential due to absence of systematic pipeline management processes.

Key Issues

  • Service quality is not the problem—operational execution is: World-class training delivery from longest-tenured team in network, but relatively minor inefficiencies compound across social media consistency, community engagement, milestone/birthday recognition, and member touchpoints
  • Absence of intentional acquisition and active retention processes: Lack of specific, systematic approaches to guide prospects through conversion journey and proactively support at-risk members is why cancellations occur and new sign-ups fail to materialise
  • Pipeline conversion gap: 190 first visits in Q4 failed to convert into sustainable membership growth due to missing systematic processes for registration → intro offer → first visit → membership conversion
  • Declining active membership base: Lost 37 active members across Q4 (206 to 169), directly compressing recurring membership revenue by $7,266 monthly from October to December—driven by operational gaps, not service quality
  • Utilisation significantly below target: 46.09% average utilisation vs 70-75% target range creates substantial capacity opportunity once membership base stabilises and grows

Active Interventions Being Deployed

  • Weekly FOH schedule with itemised daily tasks: Systematises all previously inconsistent processes—social media posting, community engagement activities, milestone/birthday recognition, member touchpoints—ensuring completion with consistency rather than ad-hoc execution when time permits
  • Acquisition and retention processes taking absolute precedence: The FOH restructure places intentional, specific acquisition workflows and active retention strategies at the centre of daily operations. This is the critical piece that must commence with urgency—comprehensive training has been completed, contract amendments have been made, and we have now reached the acceptance deadline with commencement expected immediately
  • Operational framework rollout: Systematic pipeline management processes with defined workflows for guiding prospects through each conversion stage, accountability measures for execution, and clear daily/weekly expectations for all team members involved in member-facing activities
  • Ongoing process and sales training: Continuous capability development recognising acquisition and retention represent entirely new skill sets that the team must learn, develop, and become proficient and effective in executing—sustained development process with organisational support throughout
  • Recovr retention software deployment: Automated at-risk member identification and re-engagement workflows now operational, providing systematic member support during vulnerable periods and ensuring no member slips through the cracks
  • Coordinated team initiatives: All-hands conversion efforts for intro offer prospects, FOH-led sales calls for pipeline engagement, and trainer-led at-risk member identification creating comprehensive coverage across the entire member lifecycle

Expected Outcomes

  • Reverse membership decline through systematic execution by addressing the root cause—operational inefficiencies, not service quality—ensuring consistent execution of acquisition and retention processes
  • Transform lead generation into committed memberships by converting the consistent first visit activity (190 in Q4) into sustainable membership growth through intentional pipeline management
  • Reduce cancellations through active retention strategies that proactively identify and support at-risk members before issues escalate, leveraging Recovr automation, trainer identification, and systematic FOH touchpoints
  • Rebuild recurring revenue base by stabilising and growing membership revenue stream through combined acquisition improvement and retention strengthening, reversing the $7,266 monthly compression experienced in Q4
  • Optimise capacity utilisation by driving session attendance toward 60%+ targets through membership base growth, improved booking frequency from re-engagement efforts, and stronger member stickiness from consistent community connection
  • January 2026 shows positive trajectory shift with current data (as of 22nd) showing active members increased to 180 (up from 169 in December), intro offers reached 46 (already triple December's full-month total of 15), and suspended members decreased to 65 (down from 88 as holiday travel normalised). These indicators, combined with the imminent commencement of systematic acquisition and retention processes, support the potential for sustained membership recovery. The team has world-class service delivery capability; they now have the operational systems to match it—urgent execution of these processes is the pathway forward

SUMMARY & PATH FORWARD

Wetherill Park's Q4 2025 performance demonstrates both the challenges faced and the comprehensive response now being executed to address them. The quarter ended with 257 total members (169 active), down from 269 in October, while generating $176,446 in revenue with utilisation averaging 46.09%—well below the 70-75% target range. The fundamental issue throughout Q4 was consistent underperformance in acquisition, with 23 membership sales against 36 cancellations producing net negative member movement of -13 across the quarter. While attrition rates remained within acceptable ranges (averaging 4.54%), and the team demonstrated strong execution capability through November's Black Friday package sales (81 units), the absence of systematic pipeline management processes meant that consistent first visit activity (190 in Q4) failed to convert into sustainable membership growth.

The operational framework rollout has involved comprehensive training for FOH acquisition and retention processes, with the team receiving detailed instruction on systematic pipeline management, weekly schedules with itemised daily tasks, and the specific workflows required to guide prospects through each conversion stage. However, active commencement of these responsibilities is pending contract amendments that reflect these critical additions to the FOH role—a necessary step that required demonstrating how the role would function operationally before finalising agreements. Commencement is expected in the immediate to near future, with the understanding that this represents an entirely new skill set and process that the team must learn, develop, and become not only proficient in, but effective in executing. Ongoing process and sales training continues to build capability, while Recovr retention software provides automated support for at-risk member identification and re-engagement workflows.

Current January 2026 data (as of 22nd January, not month-end totals) provides encouraging signs of early momentum. Active membership has increased from December's 169 to 180, while total membership sits at 245 with suspended members decreasing from 88 to 65 as holiday travel patterns normalised. Lead generation activity shows renewed pipeline development with 46 intro offers sold to date in January—already more than triple December's full-month total of 15—and 59 first visits recorded so far. As the FOH acquisition and retention processes commence active deployment and the team continues developing proficiency through ongoing training, these early indicators support the potential for sustained improvement. The infrastructure, systematic processes, and support systems are now in place—the focus ahead is on building execution capability through consistent practice, refinement, and organisational support as the team masters these new responsibilities.

Key Takeaways

  • Q4 ended at 257 members (169 active), $176,446 revenue, 46.09% utilisation—comprehensive response now being executed
  • FOH acquisition and retention training completed; active commencement pending contract amendments expected in immediate to near future
  • Ongoing process and sales training continues—entirely new skill set requiring development time to achieve proficiency and effectiveness
  • Current January data (as of 22nd) shows early momentum: 180 active members, 46 intro offers (vs 15 in all of Dec), 65 suspended (down from 88)
  • Infrastructure and systematic processes in place—focus is building execution capability through practice, refinement, and ongoing support
